## 1. Geometry and ambiguity

Analytic threshold for exact ambiguity: side >= 1/sqrt(3) = 0.5774 wavelengths (circumradius 1/3 wavelength). Derivation in `array_model.triangle_exact_ambiguity_threshold`.

| side | at 915 MHz | at 5.8 GHz |
|---|---|---|
| λ/3 | 10.9 cm | 1.72 cm |
| λ/2 | 16.4 cm | 2.58 cm |
| 0.577λ (R=λ/3) | 18.9 cm | 2.98 cm |
| λ | 32.8 cm | 5.17 cm |

Numerical check (pairs >= 45 deg apart, 0.5 deg grid): min D for side < 0.55 lambda is 0.010; max D for side > 0.60 lambda is 8.1e-05. Worst-case secondary lobe at side = lambda/2: -1.1 dB; at side = lambda: 0.00 dB.

## 3. Null steering

Null depth = 10 log10(|w^H a_jammer|^2 / |w^H a_target|^2): the same weights' response toward the jammer relative to the target. Weights are computed for the ideal array; errors are applied to the array they are evaluated on. Target 0 deg, jammers 70 and 200 deg unless stated. Numerical floor is -300 dB.

|---|---|---|---|---|

## 3b. MVDR with sample covariance

## 5. Narrowband validity for the LoRa source

Triangle of side lambda/2. 'B*tau' is bandwidth times the longest inter-element delay (needs << 1). 'Edge loss' is the worst-case beamforming loss (over 360 deg) at the band edge when phases are set for the carrier. 'Edge phase' is the phase error across the longest baseline at the band edge.

| signal | BW/fc | max delay | B*tau | edge loss | edge phase |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LoRa BW125k @ 915 MHz | 1.37e-04 | 546 ps | 6.8e-05 | 3.3e-08 dB | 0.012 deg |
| LoRa BW500k @ 915 MHz | 5.46e-04 | 546 ps | 2.7e-04 | 5.3e-07 dB | 0.049 deg |
| 20 MHz data @ 5.8 GHz | 3.45e-03 | 86 ps | 1.7e-03 | 2.1e-05 dB | 0.310 deg |
| 80 MHz data @ 5.8 GHz | 1.38e-02 | 86 ps | 6.9e-03 | 3.4e-04 dB | 1.241 deg |

Waveform check: an SF7 BW125k chirp delayed by the full lambda/2 baseline delay (theta = 40 deg) differs from the phase-only model by 9.5e-05 relative amplitude, and the cross-correlation phase differs from the true carrier phase by 7.4e-05 deg. A full chirp is spectrally centred, so the delay-induced phase bias is about zero.

Practical consequence: the 2-channel DOA needs no dechirping. Cross-correlating the two channels (sum of x2 * conj(x1)) cancels the chirp and keeps only the inter-channel phase. Dechirp/FFT is still useful to find packet start and to pick the samples that contain signal.
